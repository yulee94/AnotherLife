# Private Kingdom HUD — Infinity Kingdom Density Review

## Benchmark

Reference: the official Infinity Kingdom Google Play listing, especially the “Build Your Kingdom” city-management screenshot:
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gtarcade.ioe.global

The density target is the management grammar, not the franchise art: a readable resource header, contextual city actions, persistent progress/notification signals, and a compact module dock around an unobstructed city view.

## What now passes

- The 2.5D city remains visible behind the management HUD.
- Eight persistent top chips expose Food, Wood, Stone, Gold, Mana, Ore, the realm-rare resource, and Town Hall level.
- A separate build-timer strip always renders `READY`, a live countdown, or `COMPLETE`.
- Castle status remains visible beside the timer strip.
- The bounded quest tracker no longer expands into a full-screen plate or hides the management surface.
- The bottom dock exposes City, Construct `[B]`, Research, Troops, Advisors, Map, and Shared Menu.
- Research, Troops, and Advisors are visibly locked rather than pretending to be implemented.
- The private map presents player-facing Castle / Area I / Area II labels for the selected realm, never raw destination IDs, outer-realm destinations, or Warzone.
- Shared Menu is the return route to the same inner-realm 3D session.
- The production HUD contains no Duel, DemoInitializer, or Command Deck control.

## Honest gaps versus the benchmark

1. **Icon language:** this HUD is still text-heavy. Infinity Kingdom communicates resources, building actions, alerts, and module identity primarily through illustrated icons and badges.
2. **Contextual city interaction:** the city has no world-space building action bubbles, collect markers, upgrade arrows, or selection ring menu beyond the Town Hall presentation.
3. **Queue depth:** only the build timer is live. Independent research, troop-training, healing, and secondary construction queues are not implemented.
4. **Module depth:** Research, Troops, and Advisors are intentionally locked. Their buttons establish information architecture but do not yet provide management screens.
5. **Notification density:** there is no production mail/event/reward/alliance notification stack or count badges.
6. **City life:** the city is static and sparse compared with the benchmark’s animated population, traffic, production feedback, and denser landmark hierarchy.
7. **Responsive validation:** evidence is 1600×900 desktop. Mobile safe areas, touch scaling, ultrawide, and low-resolution readability remain unverified.
8. **Presentation polish:** realm architecture is distinct, but materials, lighting, iconography, and micro-animation remain below the benchmark’s shipped-product finish.
